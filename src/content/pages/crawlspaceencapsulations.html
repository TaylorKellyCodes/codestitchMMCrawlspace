Foundation Protection

Stop Raleigh’s Humidity From Destroying Your Home

Did you know that up to 50% of the air you breathe on the first floor of your home comes directly from your crawl space? In the Triangle, our high humidity and red clay soil create a "perfect storm" for moisture buildup, wood rot, and unhealthy air.

Our encapsulation process transforms your damp, dark crawl space into a clean, dry, and conditioned environment. By sealing the floor, walls, and vents, we stop the "Stack Effect," preventing humid air and soil gases from rising into your living space. This doesn't just protect your foundation; it lowers your energy bills and improves your family’s respiratory health.

Our Trustworthy Process

  • Initial Inspection & Assessment: We analyze moisture levels, check for structural rot, and identify any active mold colonies in your Raleigh or Durham home.
  • Debris Removal & Leveling: We clear out old insulation and trash, then level the soil to ensure a smooth, professional vapor barrier fit.
  • Premium Sealing: We install a heavy-duty, 20-mil antimicrobial vapor barrier, meticulously sealing all seams and wrapping every pier.
  • Environmental Control: We install a high-efficiency crawl space dehumidifier and a dedicated monitoring system so you can track your humidity levels from your phone.

Is It Time?

Signs Your Crawl Space Needs Encapsulation

Most homeowners don't think about their crawl space until it starts affecting the rest of the house. In the Triangle's humid climate, these warning signs usually show up gradually:

  • Musty odors upstairs: If the first floor of your home smells damp or musty—especially after rain—air is likely being pulled up from an unsealed crawl space.
  • Visible moisture or standing water: Damp soil, condensation on ductwork, or puddles after storms are signs that groundwater or humidity is entering the space unchecked.
  • Sagging or cupping floors: Wood absorbs moisture from below, and floor joists that sag, bounce, or feel soft are often a sign of ongoing water exposure.
  • Higher-than-expected energy bills: An open, uninsulated, or unsealed crawl space is one of the biggest sources of energy loss in older homes.
  • Pest activity: Termites, rodents, and insects are drawn to damp, dark crawl spaces—especially ones with exposed dirt floors.
  • Visible mold on joists or insulation: Discoloration or a fuzzy growth on wood framing usually means moisture levels have been elevated for a while.

What Affects Cost & Timeline

Every crawl space is different, which is why we start every job with a free, no-obligation inspection rather than a phone-quote. The main factors that go into pricing and scheduling are:

  • Square footage: Larger crawl spaces need more vapor barrier material and labor time.
  • Current condition: Spaces with old insulation, debris, or existing moisture damage require more cleanout before installation can begin.
  • Accessibility: Low clearance, tight access points, or obstructions (ductwork, plumbing) can add time to the job.
  • Added systems: A dehumidifier, sump pump, or additional drainage work adds to both cost and installation time, but also to the long-term protection of your home.

As a general guide, most residential encapsulation projects are completed in one to three days once work begins. We'll walk you through the exact scope and a written price before any work starts—no surprises.
